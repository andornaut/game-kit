## Criteria

- Form rows and the slider take the caller `refuse(reducedMotion, control)`
  through `refusal: { refuse, reducedMotion }`, called on pointer, key and drag
  refusals, with the kit leaving its own cue untouched.
- Every form-row refusal path, including pointer refusal, honours the caller
  callback.
- Without it, the kit cue stays the default and behaves as today.

## Outcome

Additive. A form row or slider without a caller `refuse` behaves as in 0.88.0.

- `runtime/web/ui/dom/slider` `SliderRefusal` (`ButtonRefusal<HTMLElement>`): `refusal` takes
  `{ cue, reducedMotion }` as before or `{ refuse, reducedMotion }`, the game's
  `refuse(reducedMotion, control)` with `reducedMotion()` read at that refusal and the slider row.
  It runs on every refusal: a mouse or pen press, a touch drag or tap, a drag the slider refuses
  part way, and ArrowLeft, ArrowRight, Home and End. The slider then never calls the kit cue's
  `refuse`, `refusing` or `stopRefusal`.
- With `cue` the slider still restarts the kit cue on each refusal, as before, so only the caller
  path goes through `refuseButton`, whose kit path leaves a playing cue to finish.
- `ButtonRefusal<Control>` and `refuseButton` take the refused element's type, `HTMLButtonElement`
  by default, so existing button, form row and pager types are unchanged.
- Form rows needed no change: a refused value or switch row already plays `refusal` through
  `refuseButton` on Enter, Space, pointer presses and holds (0.87.0). Pointer refusal calls only
  the caller's `onPointerRefusal` and never the kit cue, so a game plays its own cue there.
- Tests pin each slider and form row refusal path to the caller `refuse` with no kit cue call, and
  the kit cue paths to their 0.88.0 behaviour: the slider restarting, a row leaving a cue playing.
