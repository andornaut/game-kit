## Criteria

- A slider form row with a stepped value and a set number of steps, left and
  right keys, and drag or tap on the track.
- The value word sits in the row's value column, right-aligned on ink where
  the game asks. The row has a single 16x16 icon slot.
- Game-supplied track and thumb art at locked sizes, whole-unit geometry with
  the projection hook.
- Fill-only focus and press with forced colours, and an available or refused
  state that plays the game's refusal cue.
- Reference: Archie settings view and HUD settings slider.

## Outcome

- `runtime/ui/slider`: `sliderStepForKey` (ArrowLeft, ArrowRight, Home, End),
  `sliderThumbOffset` (whole units, a half right), `sliderStepAt` (the thumb's
  centre follows the press, held to the track), `sliderLane` (track and thumb
  centred down the lane, the odd unit below), `sliderGeometry` through an
  `ActionProjection`, and `sliderPresentation` for the DOM row. Invalid input
  throws `RangeError`.
- `runtime/web/ui/dom/slider` `createSlider` with `slider.css`: a form row in
  `createFormRows`' grid, `role="slider"` with `aria-valuemin`, `aria-valuemax`,
  `aria-valuenow` and `aria-valuetext`. The icon, label, note and value word
  share the list's columns, the value word placed as the list places a state
  word; the lane spans the row below with the game's three-slice track, fill
  and thumb art at locked sizes. Keys step it; a mouse press anywhere on the
  row drags, a touch drags after sideways travel past `slop`, taps where it
  lifts and leaves vertical travel to scrolling. A refused slider keeps its
  focus and value and plays the game's cue through `refusalCue`. Row fills
  only, with the one focus fill and forced-colours system fills.
- `formRow.css` gives a slider row `touch-action: pan-y`.
- Archie's settings sliders map onto it: knob 10x16, track 10 high with 4 unit
  caps, 20 steps, the track inset 2 from the row's content on the left, the
  value word right-aligned on ink in the value column, Muted as a refused
  state. Its A/D and H/L keys stay with the game's own key handling.
- Chibi and Ada have no settings sliders.
